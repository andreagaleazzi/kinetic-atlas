These are broad associations, not safety cutoffs, validated symptom predictions, legal limits, or toxicity diagnostic criteria. Life-threatening poisoning can occur at lower estimates.

When to get help

Suspected alcohol poisoning: call 999 (UK). Severe confusion, repeated vomiting, inability to wake, irregular or slow breathing, or seizures are urgent regardless of estimated BAC. Possible serious nicotine/caffeine poisoning, chest pain, fainting, or severe palpitations: seek urgent medical care; call 999 for life-threatening symptoms. If alcohol and sedatives/opioids are combined, danger can be greater than BAC alone suggests.

Personalise your model

More context improves interpretation; only evidence-supported parameters change numerical forecasts.

01 / Physiology & lifestyle

The alcohol distribution coefficient r is a coarse proxy for body water, not a biological sex test. Height, activity, age and tolerance are retained for context but are not inserted into unvalidated metabolism formulas.

02 / Metabolism & safety context

Pregnancy, liver illness, interacting medication and hormonal contraceptives can change pharmacokinetics considerably. The automatic half-life is a broad scenario; confirm medications with a clinician. Smoking tobacco—not nicotine alone—can alter caffeine clearance.

03 / Timing & model knobs

Initial assumptions: caffeine 5h, nicotine 2h, ethanol 0.015 % BAC/hour. The bedtime caffeine guide is a user-selected reference line, not an evidence-based threshold. Adjust half-lives for sensitivity testing, not to self-diagnose metabolism.

Model, evidence & limitations

Method transparency is part of the feature—not fine print.

What the curves mean

The alcohol line approximates blood ethanol concentration (BAC, g/dL = %); it is not a blood or breath measurement. Caffeine and nicotine lines track estimated systemic amount remaining (mg) in a simplified compartment, not a direct measurement of plasma concentration or receptor occupancy. The shaded regions are hypothetical parameter scenarios, not statistical confidence intervals. Sensitivity to substances is not determined by concentration alone.

Core calculations

UK alcohol units = volume_mL × ABV_% ÷ 1000 Ethanol mass_g ≈ volume_mL × (ABV_% ÷ 100) × 0.789 BAC_% after absorption ≈ ethanol_g ÷ (r × weight_kg × 10) BAC evolution: add modeled absorbed increments; subtract beta_%/hour to zero Caffeine/nicotine: linear 1-compartment absorption + first-order elimination Elimination k = ln(2) ÷ half_life_hours Product administration is uniform over entered intake duration.

Absorption is approximate: stomach contents, drink strength, formulation, gastric emptying, time of last dose and individual physiology matter. Alcohol clearance is modeled at a constant BAC rate; caffeine and nicotine follow exponential elimination. No attempt is made to model cotinine, acetaldehyde, active caffeine metabolites, genetic variants, withdrawal, tolerance or combined-drug interactions. For acute and chronic illness, these simplifications may be wrong.

Parameter assumptions and boundaries

Alcohol uses r = 0.68 or 0.55 (editable), clearance default 0.015 %/h, and absorption delay depending on food. Illustrative alcohol bands change r by ±0.06 and clearance roughly between 0.010 and 0.022 %/h. Caffeine typical adult terminal half-life ~5h (range often 3–7h, sometimes much longer in pregnancy/interactions), with fast oral absorption. Nicotine average terminal half-life ~2h (often 1–4h), product-dependent uptake. Bands vary half-life (0.7× to 1.5×). A cigarette typically delivers ~1–2mg systemic nicotine; pouches and vapes vary widely. Pouch absorbed % is an editable approximation and is not a validated universal fraction. Patch presets represent release of stated systemic mg over 16–24h. Coffee concentrations vary with beans, recipes, yields and brew ratios; always correct estimates against labels when possible.

How to interpret guidance

EFSA considers up to 200mg in one caffeine dose and 400mg/day generally without safety concern for healthy non-pregnant adults, and up to 200mg/day in pregnancy. These are reference assessments, not individual targets or guarantees. Around 100mg near bedtime may disrupt sleep in some adults. UK NHS advises no more than 14 alcohol units/week on a regular basis, spread over 3 or more days; no amount is completely risk free. Acute intoxication can occur at lower BAC than reference bands, with tolerance and co-exposures altering symptoms. Nicotine has no safe-intake threshold applicable to everybody. This tool cannot estimate sleep architecture, fitness to drive, toxicity, dependence, legal BAC or risk of injury.
