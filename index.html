Model, evidence & limitations

What the curves mean

The alcohol line approximates blood ethanol concentration (BAC, g/dL = %); it is not a blood or breath measurement. Caffeine and nicotine lines track estimated systemic amount remaining (mg) in a simplified compartment, not a direct measurement of plasma concentration or receptor occupancy. The shaded regions are hypothetical parameter scenarios, not statistical confidence intervals. Sensitivity to substances is not determined by concentration alone. The Combined chart draws only the central lines. In Relative mode each curve is divided by its own peak in the visible window (zero stays zero), so timing and shape compare across substances but heights do not; the real ranges are listed under the chart. In mg mode all three are shown as estimated mg in the body on a log (×10 per gridline) axis, with alcohol converted from BAC to ethanol mass by the same Widmark relation: ethanol_mg = BAC_% × r × weight_kg × 10 × 1000.

Core calculations

UK alcohol units = volume_mL × ABV_% ÷ 1000 Ethanol mass_g ≈ volume_mL × (ABV_% ÷ 100) × 0.789 BAC_% after absorption ≈ ethanol_g ÷ (r × weight_kg × 10) BAC evolution: add modeled absorbed increments; subtract beta_%/hour to zero Caffeine/nicotine: linear 1-compartment absorption + first-order elimination Elimination k = ln(2) ÷ half_life_hours Pouch absorbed_% ≈ 60 × (1 − e^(−minutes_in_mouth ÷ 25)) Product administration is uniform over entered intake duration.

Absorption is approximate: stomach contents, drink strength, formulation, gastric emptying, time of last dose and individual physiology matter. Alcohol clearance is modeled at a constant BAC rate; caffeine and nicotine follow exponential elimination. No attempt is made to model cotinine, acetaldehyde, active caffeine metabolites, genetic variants, withdrawal, tolerance or combined-drug interactions. For acute and chronic illness, these simplifications may be wrong.

Parameter assumptions and boundaries

Alcohol uses r = 0.68 or 0.55 (editable), clearance default 0.015 %/h, and absorption delay depending on food. Illustrative alcohol bands change r by ±0.06 and clearance roughly between 0.010 and 0.022 %/h. Caffeine typical adult terminal half-life ~5h (range often 3–7h, sometimes much longer in pregnancy/interactions), with fast oral absorption. Nicotine average terminal half-life ~2h (often 1–4h), product-dependent uptake. Bands vary half-life (0.7× to 1.5×). A cigarette typically delivers ~1–2mg systemic nicotine; pouches and vapes vary widely. Pouch absorbed % is an editable approximation and is not a validated universal fraction. Patch presets represent release of stated systemic mg over 16–24h. Coffee concentrations vary with beans, recipes, yields and brew ratios; always correct estimates against labels when possible.

Nicotine pouches: brand, strength & time in mouth

Brand and strength only set the label nicotine per pouch; every pouch uses the same model. Label mg is not absorbed mg: measured pouches hold roughly 80–110% of the label, and only part of it leaves the pouch. The absorbed % follows time in mouth: absorbed_% ≈ 60 × (1 − e^(−minutes ÷ 25)), about 20% at 10 min, 33% at 20, 42% at 30 and 55% at 60. Fmax = 60% and τ = 25 min are a least-squares fit to used-pouch extraction studies: Azzopardi et al. 2026 (VELO 11 mg: 28, 36, 44% after 10, 20, 30 min; 20 mg: 22, 30, 37%), Lunell et al. 2020 (ZYN 3–8 mg: 50–59% after 60 min), Knopp et al. 2022 (dry ZYN: 17% at 15 min, 56% at 60) and Mallock-Ohnesorg et al. 2024 (24–52% after 20 min). Extracted nicotine is treated as absorbed because under 5% of it was swallowed (Azzopardi 2026). Individuals range from about 10% to 67%, larger and stronger pouches release a smaller share, dry pouches start slower, and three of the four studies were industry-funded, so treat the curve as an editable starting point. It applies to pouches only, not gum, lozenges, patches, cigarettes or vapes. Changing duration recalculates absorbed %, which stays editable; saved entries keep their own %.

Brand strengths (UK, checked November 2026) come from the manufacturers' UK pages for VELO, Nordic Spirit, ZYN and XQS, and from at least two UK retailers stating mg per pouch for on!, Killa and Pablo. LOOP, and strengths quoted only in mg/g or by a single retailer, are left out. Ranges change: check the can and edit the mg if it differs.

How to interpret guidance

EFSA considers up to 200mg in one caffeine dose and 400mg/day generally without safety concern for healthy non-pregnant adults, and up to 200mg/day in pregnancy. These are reference assessments, not individual targets or guarantees. Around 100mg near bedtime may disrupt sleep in some adults. UK NHS advises no more than 14 alcohol units/week on a regular basis, spread over 3 or more days; no amount is completely risk free. Acute intoxication can occur at lower BAC than reference bands, with tolerance and co-exposures altering symptoms. Nicotine has no safe-intake threshold applicable to everybody. This tool cannot estimate sleep architecture, fitness to drive, toxicity, dependence, legal BAC or risk of injury. The Guidance panel stays hidden until something in it applies to your data: a BAC band, weekly units or caffeine reference is crossed, alcohol overlaps with caffeine or nicotine, or a profile caveat (pregnancy, liver or medication, smoking, stimulant or sleep sensitivity) meets a substance logged today or still modeled in the body (above 0.001% BAC, 10 mg caffeine or 0.1 mg nicotine).

Alcohol effect ranges & when to get help

0.00–0.019%Effects may still occur; not a clearance result
0.02–0.049%Attention, judgment & reaction time may worsen
0.05–0.079%More notable impairment; coordination may suffer
0.08–0.149%Substantial motor and cognitive impairment possible
≥ 0.15%Serious impairment and poisoning risk can rise

Estimated BAC % (g/dL). These are broad associations, not safety cutoffs, validated symptom predictions, legal limits, or toxicity diagnostic criteria. Life-threatening poisoning can occur at lower estimates.

When to get help. Suspected alcohol poisoning: call 999 (UK). Severe confusion, repeated vomiting, inability to wake, irregular or slow breathing, or seizures are urgent regardless of estimated BAC. Possible serious nicotine/caffeine poisoning, chest pain, fainting, or severe palpitations: seek urgent medical care; call 999 for life-threatening symptoms. If alcohol and sedatives/opioids are combined, danger can be greater than BAC alone suggests.
